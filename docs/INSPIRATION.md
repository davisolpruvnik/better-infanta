# Inspiration

This document expands the reference list in [DESIGN.md](./DESIGN.md). For each reference, it names what was borrowed and why.

## Globo (JN, Repórter, G1/Globonews)

**What it is.** Brazilian broadcast journalism. Jornal Nacional and Fantástico are the reference programs.

**What was borrowed.** Broadcast rhythm. The assumption that a viewer can hold several facts at once. A ticker that runs alongside a story. The sense that information is being delivered, not displayed.

**Why.** Government portals assume the reader has infinite patience and one question. Broadcast assumes the reader is on the couch with the phone in one hand. The portal should assume the same.

## O Globo (newspaper)

**What it is.** The print sister of Globo. A broadsheet.

**What was borrowed.** The newsprint substrate. Masthead logic — a page has a name, a date, and an issue, and the name is at the top. Dense columns. Headlines that carry the story, decks that carry the rest.

**Why.** The portal is not a newspaper. But it is closer to a newspaper than to a brochure. The masthead gives the page an identity and a timestamp. The density respects the reader's time.

## PT.org.br

**What it is.** The Workers' Party of Brazil's website.

**What was borrowed.** Numbered steps. Mobilization structure — "here's what you do, here's the next step, here's how to find others doing it." The sense that the reader is a participant, not a spectator.

**Why.** A service page is an instruction. The reader is trying to get something done. Numbered steps turn a paragraph of requirements into a sequence of actions.

## Gov.br

**What it is.** The Brazilian federal government's citizen portal.

**What was borrowed.** The citizen-first service taxonomy. Circular service icons. The proof that a government portal can be warm without being unserious.

**Why.** Gov.br is the counterexample to the default civic template. It shows that density and warmth are compatible. The service icons are circular because a circle reads as a category marker, not a button.

## Brazilian football clubs (Flamengo, Vasco, Corinthians, Palmeiras, Cruzeiro)

**What they are.** Four of the most widely-supported football clubs of Southeastern Brazil. Rio de Janeiro's *Flamengo* and *Vasco da Gama* (red, black), São Paulo's *Corinthians* (black, white, red) and *Palmeiras* (green, white), and Belo Horizonte's *Cruzeiro* (blue, white).

**What was borrowed.** The shades of red, green, and blue that anchor Kumpol. The clubs are mass cultural institutions in Brazil — not elite brands, but what people wear on the street. Their colors carry a sense of belonging, loyalty, and community inclusion.

**Why.** A civic portal can be visually alive. The Kumpol palette does not come from a design trend; it comes from institutions people already love and feel *saudade* (longing/nostalgia). The hues are translated into named tokens (Dugo, Ampiyas, Pakatan, Tihim, Semento) and assigned functional roles, so the energy is kept and the meaning is controlled.

## TF1 (election graphics)

**What it is.** French television election coverage.

**What was borrowed.** Composition as information. The layout itself tells the result before the viewer reads a number. The council composition grid on the portal uses this logic — the Vice Mayor is top center, the eight councilors sit in two rows, the two ex-officio members sit below. The position on the grid is the information.

**Why.** Some information is spatial. A list of names does not convey a council's structure. A grid does.

## Le Monde

**What it is.** French daily newspaper, known for cartography.

**What was borrowed.** Cartographic clarity in its 2024 presidential election website. The barangay map uses this — labels are placed where they do not obscure, boundaries are drawn at a consistent weight, and the map is legible in one color.

**Why.** A map is not a decoration. It is a way to answer "where." If the reader has to zoom to read a label, the map has failed.

## T13 (Chile)

**What it is.** Chilean television network.

**What was borrowed.** Numeral treatment. Restraint in the broadcast register. Numbers on T13 are large, tabular, and never accompanied by a "wow" graphic.

**Why.** The portal has numbers. The numbers should be readable and unadorned. Restraint is what makes a number legible.

## Other references considered

**GOV.UK and USWDS.** Both are well-known civic design systems and both were studied. Neither became a primary reference. GOV.UK is optimized for a desktop reader with time; BetterInfanta is optimized for a phone reader in line at the municipal hall. USWDS is designed by and for a government; BetterInfanta is not the government.

**Kapwa.** A Philippine design system used in BetterGovPH. Studied and respected. The naming of the color and type systems in Filipino follows Kapwa's example.
